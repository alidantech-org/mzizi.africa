# WHY — Why Mzizi Africa Exists

## The short answer

Mzizi Africa exists because African history, heritage, geography, culture, language, governance and politics are deeply connected, but the evidence describing them is fragmented, inconsistently structured, difficult to verify and often presented through modern categories that hide historical change.

Mzizi researches that material, preserves the evidence, structures the knowledge cleanly and makes the relationships explorable across **place, time, people, institutions and sources**.

## The problem

A person trying to understand one African place may need to search across:

- oral-history collections;
- books and academic papers;
- museum and archive catalogues;
- colonial-era documents;
- constitutions and legislation;
- government portals;
- election records;
- census and statistical publications;
- maps from different eras;
- language and ethnographic research;
- procurement and finance datasets;
- news reporting;
- international organization records;
- scanned PDFs and image-only documents.

These sources were created for different purposes, use different names, contradict each other, describe different time periods and frequently assume different boundaries.

A normal search engine can find documents. A normal database can store rows. A normal museum can present selected narratives. A normal political dashboard can visualize current statistics.

**Mzizi's reason for existing is to connect these functions into one evidence-first knowledge system.**

## The common why

Every major feature in Mzizi should serve at least one part of this chain:

```text
Research
  -> Preserve evidence
  -> Extract information
  -> Clean and classify it
  -> Evaluate confidence and disagreement
  -> Structure relationships
  -> Version change through time
  -> Publish understandable knowledge
  -> Connect history to the present
```

If a proposed feature does not strengthen this chain, its place in Mzizi should be questioned.

## Why heritage and culture belong here

African heritage is not decoration around political history. Material culture, oral tradition, architecture, belief systems, artistic traditions, social institutions, memory and identity provide essential evidence about how societies understood themselves.

Mzizi should function partly as a digital heritage museum: not merely showing objects or stories, but connecting them to the places, peoples, languages, periods and political systems that produced them.

This layer gives meaning to otherwise abstract maps and timelines.

## Why history belongs here

The present cannot be understood without chronology.

Borders move. Kingdoms expand and decline. Names change. Institutions are created, abolished and transformed. Communities migrate. Trade routes rise and fall. Colonial administrations redraw geography. Independence creates new states. Constitutions alter political authority.

Mzizi therefore treats historical validity as a core property, not as a note attached to current data.

## Why geography belongs here

Modern maps are snapshots, not eternal truth.

Using only today's national borders can distort pre-colonial, colonial and even recent history. Mzizi must represent:

- historical polities;
- kingdoms and empires;
- chiefdoms and community territories;
- colonial possessions and protectorates;
- historical provinces and districts;
- disputed boundaries;
- changing counties, regions, constituencies and wards;
- overlapping cultural, linguistic and political geographies.

Geography is the spatial index through which much of the rest of Mzizi becomes understandable.

## Why linguistics belongs here

Language carries evidence about identity, contact, movement and historical relationships.

Language families, dialect continuums, borrowed vocabulary, historical names and language shifts can illuminate migration, trade, political expansion and cultural exchange.

Mzizi should not present language as a static label on a modern country. It should be possible to study language across time and geography and connect linguistic evidence to other historical claims.

## Why migration belongs here

People move, settle, mix, disperse and return.

Migration helps explain:

- language distribution;
- settlement patterns;
- cultural exchange;
- political formation;
- conflict and alliance;
- trade networks;
- diaspora relationships;
- colonial labor systems;
- urbanization and modern demographic change.

Migration is therefore a relationship over time, not simply a demographic statistic.

## Why colonial influence belongs here

Colonial rule changed borders, administrative systems, economies, languages, education, land regimes, legal systems and political institutions across Africa.

Mzizi should document colonial influence without treating colonialism as the beginning of African history.

The system should make it possible to compare:

```text
pre-colonial structures
       -> colonial transformation
       -> independence transition
       -> post-colonial institutions
       -> present-day consequences
```

That chain is central to understanding why many current political and geographic structures look the way they do.

## Why governance belongs here

Governance explains how authority is organized and exercised.

Mzizi should make institutions understandable by connecting:

- constitutional authority;
- laws;
- branches of government;
- ministries and agencies;
- national and subnational offices;
- office holders;
- appointments;
- public responsibilities;
- institutional changes over time.

A title such as "President", "Governor", "Chief", "District Commissioner" or "Prime Minister" has meaning only within a particular legal, political and historical system.

## Why politics belongs here

Governance describes formal institutions. Politics describes competition, organization, ideology and negotiation around power.

Mzizi therefore needs political parties, movements, coalitions, candidates, manifestos, elections and political relationships alongside formal governance structures.

This allows the platform to show not only who legally holds authority, but how they reached power, what they promised and how political organization changed.

## Why law belongs here

Constitutions and laws define much of the formal authority in modern states.

Mzizi should connect legal provisions to the institutions and offices they create or constrain. Legal text should also be versioned so users can understand what the law said at a particular time rather than seeing only today's text.

## Why public finance, debt and procurement belong here

Power is not only institutional; it is exercised through resources.

Budgets, revenue, expenditure, borrowing, tenders and contracts reveal what governments prioritize and how public authority becomes material action.

Connecting political institutions to money enables questions such as:

- What budget did this administration control?
- What projects were funded in this region?
- How did debt change under a government?
- Which tenders or contracts followed a public policy decision?

This turns Mzizi from a descriptive political database into a governance transparency platform.

## Why evidence and provenance belong everywhere

Political and historical information can be disputed, incomplete or manipulated.

For that reason, Mzizi must preserve:

- source identity;
- source artifact;
- retrieval time;
- author/publisher where known;
- extraction method;
- transformations performed;
- confidence;
- disagreement;
- reviewer decisions;
- record versions.

Evidence is not a separate "sources page" added after the product is complete. It is part of the data architecture.

## Why AI belongs here

The volume and complexity of source material make purely manual processing too slow.

AI can help with:

- recognizing entities;
- classifying records;
- matching alternate names;
- comparing documents;
- cleaning extracted text;
- proposing relationships;
- detecting contradictions;
- evaluating extraction quality;
- summarizing evidence for reviewers.

But AI must not become an invisible authority. Its outputs should be attributable, reviewable and versioned.

The purpose of AI in Mzizi is **to accelerate research and stewardship without weakening evidence standards**.

## Why the platform matters now

Mzizi's long-term value is that it can connect a heritage question to a modern political question without switching to an unrelated system.

A user could begin with:

> What communities and political entities existed around this region before colonial rule?

and continue into:

> How did colonial administration change the boundaries?

then:

> How did those boundaries influence the modern county or state?

then:

> Who governs it today, under which law, and what public resources are allocated there?

That continuity is the defining reason for Mzizi.

## The standard for success

Mzizi succeeds when a user can explore an African claim and understand:

1. **what happened;**
2. **where it happened;**
3. **when it was true;**
4. **who or what was involved;**
5. **how it relates to other historical/current structures;**
6. **which evidence supports it;**
7. **how certain or disputed it is;**
8. **how the information changed over time.**

The platform should make Africa's historical depth and present-day complexity more legible without pretending that uncertain history is simple.
