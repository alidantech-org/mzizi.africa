# STORY — The Mzizi Africa Story

## Mzizi means roots

Mzizi begins with a simple idea: the present has roots.

A modern African border has roots in older political geography. A language map has roots in migration and contact. A presidency has roots in constitutions, colonial administration, independence settlements and political struggle. A public budget has roots in institutions, elections and promises. A cultural object has roots in a community, place, period, belief system and network of exchange.

Most digital products show only one slice of that story.

Mzizi is built to follow the roots.

## The experience we want to create

Imagine opening Mzizi and selecting a place in Africa.

The first thing you see is not simply today's country profile. You can move backward through time.

The map changes.

Modern counties disappear. Colonial districts appear. Earlier polities, kingdoms, city-states, communities, trade networks and spheres of influence become visible where evidence allows them to be reconstructed.

The platform tells you that a boundary is approximate when it is approximate. It shows competing interpretations when sources disagree. It lets you inspect the evidence behind a claim.

You can then move sideways rather than only backward.

Select a language and see where related languages are documented. Follow migration evidence. Explore trade connections. Open a heritage object, oral tradition, place name or historical event and see how it relates to the region.

Then move forward again.

See colonial administrative changes. Trace independence. Open the constitution. See the government structure that followed. See political offices, parties, elections and leaders. Follow public finance, debt, procurement and major government actions into the present.

The user has moved from heritage to politics without leaving the same knowledge system.

That is Mzizi.

## A digital heritage museum — but not only a museum

One face of Mzizi should feel like a living African heritage museum.

It should allow people to explore:

- peoples and communities;
- cultural practices;
- historical places;
- material heritage;
- architecture;
- oral traditions;
- languages;
- historical names;
- kingdoms, states and political systems;
- trade and migration;
- maps and timelines;
- source documents and archival material.

But Mzizi should not freeze heritage in the past.

A museum object can connect to a language. The language can connect to a migration. The migration can connect to a polity. The polity can connect to colonial transformation. Colonial transformation can connect to a modern border or institution.

Heritage becomes a route into understanding the current world.

## A public record — but not only a political database

Another face of Mzizi is a structured public record.

A user should eventually be able to explore a country or local area and understand:

- its constitutional history;
- its current and historical government structure;
- its political offices;
- office holders and appointments;
- political parties and coalitions;
- elections, candidates and results;
- manifestos and political promises;
- budgets and expenditure;
- debt;
- tenders and contracts;
- major public events and decisions.

But this information should not appear as if modern governance emerged from nowhere.

Mzizi's difference is the historical chain behind it.

## A research platform — not just a presentation layer

Behind the public experience is a serious research system.

Researchers and data stewards need to be able to:

1. register or discover a source;
2. preserve the source artifact;
3. extract text, tables, maps or metadata;
4. classify what the source contains;
5. link entities and alternate names;
6. compare the extraction to existing knowledge;
7. flag contradictions or uncertainty;
8. review AI suggestions;
9. promote accepted records into canonical data;
10. preserve every important version and decision.

The dashboard is therefore not simply an admin panel. It is the workbench where raw evidence becomes trustworthy structured knowledge.

## Example story: one place across time

A user searches for **Mombasa**.

Mzizi can eventually tell multiple connected stories:

### Geography

Where was the settlement located? How was the surrounding territory described in different periods? Which modern administrative units now contain it?

### Trade

Which Indian Ocean trade routes connected it to other African ports, Arabia, Persia, India and beyond?

### Language and culture

Which languages and cultural communities are documented in the region, and how did contact influence vocabulary, identity and material culture?

### Political history

Which political authorities governed or influenced the settlement in different periods?

### Colonial transformation

How did Portuguese, Omani, British or other external powers alter administration, trade or political relationships over time?

### Modern governance

How is the area governed today? Which constitutional and statutory structures define that authority?

### Politics and public resources

Who represents the area? What elections produced those representatives? What public budgets, development indicators or procurement records relate to it?

### Evidence

Which sources support each part of the story, and which claims are contested?

One place becomes an entry point into a connected African knowledge graph.

## Example story: one institution across time

A user searches for a modern office such as a presidency, governorship or ministry.

Instead of returning only the current office holder, Mzizi should allow the user to ask:

- When was the office created?
- Which constitution or law gives it authority?
- Did an earlier colonial or pre-independence institution perform similar functions?
- How have the office's powers changed?
- Who held it at different times?
- Were holders elected or appointed?
- Which parties or political movements were involved?
- Which budgets or public actions fall under the institution?

The office becomes a historical object, not just a row in a current directory.

## Example story: a language as evidence

A user selects a language family.

Mzizi can connect:

```text
language evidence
    -> documented locations
    -> historical names and variants
    -> migration hypotheses
    -> neighboring language contact
    -> trade routes
    -> communities
    -> political entities
    -> colonial language policy
    -> modern official-language status
```

The platform should distinguish documented facts from scholarly hypotheses and show the evidence behind both.

## Example story: from election promise to public action

A modern user opens an election.

They can inspect:

- candidates;
- parties;
- results;
- manifestos;
- geographic voting patterns;
- offices being contested.

After the election, Mzizi can connect the winner to:

- office tenure;
- legal authority;
- budgets;
- debt decisions;
- procurement;
- major policies or projects;
- later election outcomes.

This is where historical intelligence becomes governance accountability.

## The two major user modes

### Explore mode

For the public, students, diaspora communities, educators, journalists and curious users.

The emphasis is:

- maps;
- timelines;
- stories;
- evidence cards;
- heritage collections;
- country/place profiles;
- comparisons;
- clear explanations of uncertainty.

This is primarily `apps/www`.

### Research and stewardship mode

For researchers, editors, data stewards, partner institutions and internal teams.

The emphasis is:

- source ingestion;
- extraction review;
- entity resolution;
- data quality;
- AI-assisted classification;
- source comparison;
- contradiction handling;
- temporal/version review;
- canonical publication;
- audit history.

This is primarily `apps/dashboard` plus the supporting services.

## What Mzizi should never become

Mzizi should not become:

- a collection of uncited AI-generated history;
- a political news feed with no durable data model;
- a modern-country database pretending modern borders explain all historical reality;
- a static museum disconnected from contemporary Africa;
- a dashboard of charts without source provenance;
- a scraper that accumulates unreviewed data;
- a single giant API service owning every system responsibility.

## The long-term story

The long-term product is a shared African knowledge infrastructure where a researcher, student, journalist or citizen can begin with almost any meaningful entity — a place, person, language, kingdom, event, law, office, election, budget or cultural artifact — and move through its relationships across time.

The public experience should feel intuitive and visual.

The underlying system should be rigorous, source-aware and versioned.

That combination is the product story:

> **Mzizi makes Africa's roots explorable, and uses those roots to make the present more understandable.**
