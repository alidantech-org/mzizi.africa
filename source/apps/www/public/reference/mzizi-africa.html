Culture & practice

Not everything worth recording was built in stone

Divination systems, tunings, weaving grammars, smelting rites, board games. These are the hardest records to date and the easiest to flatten — so each one says plainly how we know it.

Historical linguistics

Languages are evidence, and they are dated differently

Where a word was borrowed, and from whom, can date a contact that left no pottery. Turn on the language layer in the map to see reconstructed distributions and the Bantu expansion routes.

What this evidence can and cannot do

  • It can show contact. A borrowed word for an iron tool travels with the tool, and it dates the meeting of two communities even when neither wrote anything down.
  • It can order events. Which sound change happened first is recoverable. That gives a sequence — reliable — before it gives any calendar date.
  • It cannot give firm dates alone. Glottochronology assumes languages change at a steady rate. They do not. Dates here come from archaeology; linguistics supplies the branching.
  • A family is not a people. Shared language does not mean shared ancestry, shared culture or a single migration. The Bantu expansion moved languages, farming and iron in different combinations in different places.
  • Classification is argued. Nilo-Saharan in particular is a proposal, not a settled unit. The map draws it dashed for that reason.
Method

The hard part was never the software

It is deciding what counts as knowing. Four rules hold the whole record together.

One

A claim, not a fact

Nothing is stored as a plain statement. Each claim carries its period, its place, its sources and its confidence — and can be argued with on its own terms.

Two

Confidence never inherits

A well-excavated city can hold a completely speculative claim. Certainty attaches to the sentence, never to the site, the source, or the civilisation.

Three

Sources keep their limits

Every source is listed with what it cannot tell you. A traveller who never arrived, a chronicle copied three centuries later, a survey that opened four trenches in a square kilometre.

Four

Disagreement is content

Where specialists differ, both readings are published side by side with what would settle it. A single tidy answer would be the least honest thing on the page.

Coverage

One region at a time, properly

A continent is not a subject. Each region carries its own specialists, its own source languages and its own arguments.

Hold a source we don't?

Excavation reports, family archives, recorded oral testimony, wordlists, survey data, photographs of inscriptions. Contributors keep authorship, and every record shows who added what.
